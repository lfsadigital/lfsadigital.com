What to check before you buy

  • Know who fixes the workflow when an integration changes.
  • Choose one recurring job you can describe and review.
  • Count your setup and maintenance time alongside the software bill.
  • Agree on what the system can do before giving it access.
  • Measure recovered hours and additional sales separately. Revenue still has costs attached.

Start with the job you want done.

A client sends documents. Someone checks them, spots what's missing and writes the follow-up. That's a job we can define and build.

AI can help with the document checks and the draft. The workflow still needs rules for handling problems and someone to check the work.

At LFSA, an AI employee is one specific role built around that process. We start with drafts and give it more access with your approval.

2. DIY automation tools

Best when you want to own the setup.

If someone on your team wants to build the workflow, a platform gives them the pieces. Zapier and n8n are two examples worth looking at.

Check the integrations you need and how usage is billed. Then decide who will test the workflow and handle a failed run. Put that time in your budget.

Self-hosting adds server and security work. n8n's documentation explains the technical knowledge required. Pick that route when you have someone who can take it on.

Who I'd recommend it to: A team with the time and skills to own the workflow. Start with a task you can test and undo.

3. A freelance builder

Best for a defined project with handover.

You know what needs to happen and want someone to build it. A freelancer can fit that job. The agreement matters as much as the demo.

Write down what will be built, how it will be tested and what you receive at the end. Ask who owns the accounts and who will support the workflow once the project ends. Include any ongoing maintenance in the quote.

Who I'd recommend it to: A business buying one defined project with a plan for running it afterwards.

We also do one-time workflow builds with documentation and handover when ongoing operation isn't what you need.
